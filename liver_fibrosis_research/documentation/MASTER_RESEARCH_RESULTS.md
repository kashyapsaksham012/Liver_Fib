## 1. Project Overview & Governing Execution Rules

This document serves as the single, authoritative reference for the entire non-invasive liver fibrosis research project. It consolidates every numerical finding, statistical test, dataset count, calibration metric, fairness disparity, conformal coverage rate, mitigation outcome, diagnostic finding, sensitivity analysis, and external validation search result from the inception of the project to its final frozen state (`git HEAD: 142595d`).

### Governing Execution Rules
1. **Source of Truth:** Every number and conclusion is derived strictly from verified repository parquet/CSV artifacts and python scripts.
2. **Disambiguation & Lineage:** Exploratory, historical, superseded, and invalid analyses are preserved for scientific transparency but explicitly demarcated to prevent contamination of manuscript reporting.
3. **No Unsubstantiated Interpretations:** Negative, neutral, and edge-case results (e.g., LightGBM prespecified coverage shortfall of 0.54 pp, lack of external validation dataset, specificity cost of fairness) are documented with full fidelity.

## 5. SECTION 4: WHAT TO WRITE IN THE PAPER

This section contains ONLY conclusions directly supported by the 60 experiments. No recommendations, outside hypotheses, or unexecuted experiments are included.

### 5.1 Primary Discrimination & Model Equivalence
- Non-invasive routine blood laboratory tests combined with age and sex achieve modest to strong discrimination for F2+ significant liver fibrosis (`LUXSMED >= 8.2 kPa`) in a representative US adult population (AUROC 0.8229 to 0.8429, PR-AUC 0.3501 to 0.3705).
- Discrimination performance is statistically indistinguishable across linear (Logistic Regression), tree ensemble (Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM), and neural network (MLP) architectures under primary FDR correction.
- XGBoost achieved the highest point estimates for AUROC (0.8429) and PR-AUC (0.3705), but did not reach statistically significant superiority over other models in primary test set comparisons.

### 5.2 Calibration & Class-Balancing Prior Shift
- Standard class-balancing techniques (`class_weight='balanced'`, `scale_pos_weight`) introduce severe probability inflation and prior-shift miscalibration in raw model outputs (ECE 0.1245 to 0.2814).
- Out-of-fold Platt scaling successfully eliminates prior-shift miscalibration across all 5 model families (test ECE 0.0138 to 0.0181, Brier score 0.0669 to 0.0691) while preserving exact AUROC discrimination.

### 5.3 Demographic Fairness & The Two-Mechanism Rule
- Routine-lab machine learning models exhibit severe, systemic sensitivity underperformance in normal-weight individuals (Normal-BMI sensitivity 0.4762 to 0.6190 vs Obese sensitivity 0.8182 to 0.9535; gap -27.06 to -47.73 percentage points, p < 0.001).
- Older adults (Age 60+) experience statistically significant sensitivity degradation across 4 out of 5 model families (RF, XGB, LGBM, MLP; sensitivity gap -11.18 to -14.72 percentage points, FDR q < 0.05).
- Demographic disparities are governed by two distinct mathematical mechanisms:
  1. **BMI Disparity is Threshold-Driven:** Shifting decision thresholds halves the Normal-BMI sensitivity gap (gap shrinks by 49% to 69%), proving that normal-weight individuals produce systematically lower risk scores.
  2. **Age Disparity is Non-Threshold-Driven:** Shifting decision thresholds actively worsens Age 60+ sensitivity gaps across all 5 models (gap widens by 26% to 133%), disproving threshold misplacement as the cause of age degradation.

### 5.4 Conformal Prediction, Subgroup Failure & Intersectional Validity
- Global split conformal prediction ($\alpha = 0.10$) satisfies mathematical marginal coverage validity (~90.0% coverage overall), but systematically fails subgroup coverage in Normal-BMI (81.04% to 82.65%) and Age 60+ (84.40% to 85.80%) individuals.
- Global split conformal prediction suffers catastrophic coverage collapse in 2-way intersectional subgroups (Obese AND Age 60+, baseline coverage 64.97% to 75.17%).
- **Calibration Does Not Imply Conformal Coverage:** Subgroup Platt scaling fails to alter Brier scores or restore conformal coverage, whereas re-deriving nonconformity quantiles restores Obese-BMI coverage to 89.9%–91.6% without changing probability calibration. Conformal validity depends on residual variance, not probability calibration.
- **FDR-Gated Mondrian Conformal Prediction:** Group-conditional Mondrian calibration restores coverage in targeted single-attribute subgroups for 4 out of 5 models, but creates an overall coverage tolerance breach in XGBoost (93.85% overall coverage vs <= 93.0% limit) and fails to resolve the true 2-way intersection.
- **Prespecified M4b Joint Conformal Shrinkage ($N_0^* = 0$):** Deriving conformal quantiles exclusively on calibration data restores test-set intersectional coverage to >= 90.0% for 4 out of 5 models (LR 90.82%, RF 92.86%, XGB 91.50%, MLP 90.82%), with LightGBM achieving 89.46% (misses target by 0.54 pp / 2 cases due to tree histogram binning discretization).
- **Efficiency Cost:** Prespecified M4b calibration achieves intersectional coverage guarantees with minimal efficiency loss, increasing mean prediction set size from 1.10 to 1.19 items (+0.09 items/patient) and maintaining an 81% singleton rate.

### 5.5 Demographic Transportability & Study Limitations
- Models trained on non-Black populations experience significant transportability loss when evaluated on an unrepresented Non-Hispanic Black holdout cohort, manifested by a 0.05–0.06 drop in AUROC (0.7719 to 0.7893) and severe calibration drift.
- Complete-case missing lab exclusion disproportionately affected Non-Hispanic Black participants (41.3% of missing lab cases vs 25.0% of retained cases), establishing missingness control as a critical demographic auditing step.
- Independent external validation was non-feasible due to the absence of publicly available datasets combining VCTE elastography (`LUXSMED >= 8.2 kPa`) and matching routine blood laboratory panels.
